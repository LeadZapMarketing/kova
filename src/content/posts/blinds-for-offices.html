The short answer: for most office windows, aluminium Venetian blinds work best, because you tilt the slats to take the glare off the screen instead of closing the whole window. For a meeting room, timber Venetian blinds give the same control with a warmer look. Where you want one clean sheet of fabric, a sunscreen or dim-out roller blind cuts the glare without heavy curtains. This guide explains how to choose office blinds for glare, privacy and easy care.

Why glare is the first thing to solve

Sunlight reflecting off a laptop or monitor makes work uncomfortable. When an office or a home office first messages us, the problem can sound like this: "My office screen gets too much glare."

Workplace rules in Europe and the UK say the same thing: windows at a screen workstation should be fitted with an adjustable covering that cuts down the daylight falling on it (see the sources below). In practice that means a blind you can adjust through the day, not one that is only fully open or fully closed.

Venetian blinds: the strongest choice for offices

Our own room guide pairs offices with aluminium Venetian blinds. Screens and direct sunlight don't get along, and when glare hits the monitor you change the slat angle instead of making the whole workspace dark. On a typical window, the day can look like this:

  • 9 AM: slats open, room fully lit, view intact.
  • 12 PM: angled slightly, overhead sun softened.
  • 3 PM: tilted against the west sun, glare off the screen.
  • 8 PM: closed, privacy without drawing a curtain.

Why aluminium for a working office:

  • Easy to wipe down. Slim aluminium slats wipe clean easily, which matters in a busy office.
  • A clean, structured look. The horizontal lines suit a professional, focused space, and matte aluminium can look excellent in a monochrome office.
  • Privacy from the corridor. Tilt the slats so people walking past see less of the room while daylight still comes in. This helps on windows facing office walkways or other busy areas.

Meeting rooms: timber Venetian blinds

For a meeting room, our room guide pairs timber Venetian blinds: tilt the slats and dim the room without making it a production. Timber and faux-timber slats are wider than aluminium and give a warmer, more decorative look, which suits meeting rooms and study spaces with timber furniture.

Roller blinds: sunscreen or dim-out

If you prefer one clean sheet of fabric to slats, a roller blind cuts glare quickly without adding heavy curtains. It sits neatly against the window and takes up very little visual space. Choose the fabric by how much light you want to keep:

  • Sunscreen: an open-weave fabric that filters strong sunlight while keeping some view through the window. Use it when you still want daylight, for example on large office windows.
  • Dim-out: sits between sunscreen and blackout. Choose it for stronger glare and more privacy, while the room keeps some natural brightness.

One thing to know about sunscreen fabric: how much you can see through it depends on how open the weave is, its colour and the lighting. At night, when the office is brighter than outside, it gives less privacy than in the day. If a room needs to go properly dark, a blackout roller blind does that better than a Venetian.

Glass walls and sliding doors

Some offices and home offices have a full-height glass wall or a sliding door. Our VertiSheer blinds are made for large windows, sliding doors and glass walls: open the vanes for soft daylight, turn them for privacy or less direct sun, and draw the whole blind aside when you want the door fully open.

Home offices

A study or home office has the same problem as a company office: a screen near a bright window. The same choices apply. Aluminium Venetian blinds let you angle the light as the sun moves; a dim-out roller blind suits a home office that also needs more privacy.

Fitting a whole office

One bedroom window is a different job from furnishing a whole office. The number of windows, their sizes and the installation conditions all change the specification, so every quote is made for the actual space. We measure each window on site and check whether an inside mount (inside the window recess) or an outside mount (above and past the opening) works better. An inside mount gives a neat, built-in look when the recess is deep enough. An outside mount covers beyond the window opening, which helps when you want fewer light gaps at the sides.

Venetian blinds can also be motorised for suitable configurations; the best setup depends on the window's size, its location and how you prefer to operate the blinds.
