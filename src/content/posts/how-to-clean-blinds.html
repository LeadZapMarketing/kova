The short answer: dust your blinds once a week, spot-clean marks as soon as you see them, and never soak a fabric blind. How you do each step depends on the type of blind, because fabric, aluminium and wood react to water very differently. This guide walks through the right method for roller blinds, Venetian blinds and VertiSheer vertical sheer blinds, plus what to watch for in a humid Malaysian home.

Before you start

  • Check the care label or your order notes. Fabrics differ: a blackout fabric has a coated back, a sunscreen fabric is an open weave. When in doubt, use the gentlest method below.
  • Lower or close the blind fully so you clean the whole surface, not just the part that shows.
  • Tie up any cords or chains out of the way (and out of children's reach) while you work.
  • Motorised blinds: don't run the motor while cleaning, and keep water away from the motor and its wiring.

What you need

  • A microfibre cloth or a soft duster
  • A vacuum cleaner with a soft brush attachment, on low suction
  • Mild dishwashing liquid and lukewarm water
  • A clean, dry towel

Skip bleach, solvents, scouring pads and steam cleaners: they can fade fabric, strip coatings and warp slats.

How to clean roller blinds

Roller blinds are one flat sheet of fabric, so they are the quickest to look after.

  1. Dust weekly. Lower the blind all the way and go over it from top to bottom with a microfibre cloth, or with the vacuum's soft brush on low suction.
  2. Spot-clean marks. Mix a few drops of mild dishwashing liquid into lukewarm water. Dip a cloth, wring it out until it is only damp, and dab the mark gently. Dab, don't rub: rubbing can leave a shiny patch or a ring on the fabric.
  3. Rinse and dry. Dab the same spot with a cloth dampened in clean water, then press with a dry towel. Leave the blind fully lowered until it is completely dry before rolling it up, so it doesn't trap moisture or crease.

Blackout fabrics: be extra gentle on the coated back. Never fold, scrub or soak it, as that can crack the coating and let pinholes of light through.

How to clean Venetian blinds

Venetian blinds collect dust on every slat, so the trick is to clean each side in one pass.

  1. Close the slats one way and dust from top to bottom with a microfibre cloth or the vacuum brush. Then tilt them the other way and repeat for the back.
  2. For a deeper clean, wrap a cloth around your hand, or run a damp cloth along each slat, holding it gently between your fingers.

Aluminium slats

Aluminium handles water well. Wipe each slat with a cloth dampened in lukewarm water and a little mild detergent, then wipe again with a dry cloth so no water spots are left. Kitchen blinds pick up a light film of grease: the same mild soap solution lifts it, just change the water more often.

Wood and faux wood slats

  • Real wood: keep it dry. Dust regularly, and if a slat needs more, use a cloth that is barely damp and dry it straight away. Soaking or wet cloths can make wood swell, warp or lose its finish.
  • Faux wood: tolerates a damp cloth and mild soap better than real wood. Still dry each slat afterwards.

How to clean VertiSheer and other sheer vertical blinds

VertiSheer vanes are soft fabric, so treat them like a delicate roller fabric rather than hard slats.

  1. Dust gently with the vacuum's soft brush on the lowest setting, working from top to bottom, or use a clean microfibre duster.
  2. Spot-clean any mark with a barely damp cloth and mild soap solution: dab, don't rub, then dab with clean water.
  3. Let the vanes dry hanging straight before you open or stack them, so they keep their shape.
  4. Keep the track clear. Wipe the head rail with a dry cloth now and then so the vanes keep gliding smoothly, especially on sliding doors.

Keeping blinds fresh in Malaysia's humidity

  • Let air move. Open the blind and the window for a while each day where you can, especially in bathrooms and kitchens.
  • Never roll up or stack a damp blind. Moisture trapped in a rolled fabric is how spots and smells start.
  • Act early on spots. Small marks are far easier to lift in the first days than once they have set into the fabric.
  • Pick the right material for wet rooms. Real wood is best kept to dry rooms; for kitchens and bathrooms, aluminium or faux wood usually copes better with moisture.

How often should you clean blinds?

  • Weekly: a quick dust, a couple of minutes per window.
  • Monthly: a closer look at kitchen, bathroom and street-facing windows, with a damp wipe where needed.
  • As needed: spot-clean stains as soon as you see them.

When to replace instead of clean

If a fabric blind is badly stained, its coating is cracking, the slats are bent or warped, or the mechanism no longer holds, cleaning won't bring it back. That is a good moment to choose a blind that suits the room better, such as a blackout roller for a bedroom or aluminium Venetian slats for a kitchen.
